# Embodied Inner Union — Owner Teaching and Practice Draft

## Teaching sequence
The owner teaches Inner Union as an embodied experiential exercise rather than beginning with an imagined masculine figure. Masculine/feminine are complementary symbolic qualities present within a person, not gender prescriptions.

1. Arrive through comfortable natural breathing and bodily awareness; do not require intense breathwork.
2. Rest in witness consciousness: observing sensations, thoughts and emotions without suppression or immediate reaction. This is the experiential **inner masculine** in this model.
3. Sense spine and physical structure: the **riverbanks** offering steadiness and containment.
4. Shift into feeling, breath, sensations, movement, emotional flow and creative aliveness: the **river**, experiential **inner feminine**.
5. Alternate between observing and feeling. Eventually experience both together, not as rigidly separate identities.
6. Invite inner masculine to witness, protect and cherish feminine expression; feminine is allowed to relax, feel, play, create and receive.
7. Translate the experience into one small choice of self-respect, care or boundary in daily life.

## Daily 2–3 minute reset (draft for future audio)
Find a comfortable position. Breathe naturally and feel the support beneath you. Notice your spine, the steady axis of your body. Allow yourself to observe whatever is here without needing to change it.

Now notice the river of your experience: sensations, feelings, energy, desires, the movement of breath. Nothing needs to be perfect.

Silently invite: "My inner masculine, I invite you to protect and cherish the feminine within me—not by controlling her feelings, but by holding her with presence, dignity, and love."

And to your inner feminine: "You don't have to earn your place here. You are precious exactly as you are. You are free to feel, create, play, rest, desire, and receive."

Let the river flow within its banks. Feel steadiness and aliveness together.

"Together, we choose love without abandoning ourselves."

Ask: "What is one small action today that would honor both my tenderness and my strength?"
